Noir & Co Enterprises · Microburbs hackathon, Task 3 · 7 November 2026

Can a listing show what it leaves out, for the exact home?

Yes: on 8 of 10 untested listings our Chrome extension found the right answer, and it never showed a wrong home.

It puts Microburbs facts beside listings on realestate.com.au and Domain: the last sale updated to today's prices, daily life in walking minutes, and things to check before you buy. On the other two listings it asked the buyer to pick, with the right record on the list. We have fixed both since.

What we found along the way

109 vs 34

In Microburbs' own test on 227 flat sales, a flat's last sale updated for price changes since came within 10% of the real price 109 times. Their valuation did so 34 times, and it exists for only about 13% of City of Sydney flats. So flats lead with the updated last sale. We used these figures as given and did not rerun that test.

99.5%

That's how many of 200 random homes have heritage within 200 m, so a "nearby" flag told buyers nothing. Only 38.5% have it on the property, so that's when we flag it. The all-clear line went from 0.5% of homes to 30%.

80 places

That's all the nearby-amenities list returns, of any kind. At 2 Sam Sing Street, Waterloo, all 80 were within 372 m and just 1 was a supermarket, though its own count shows 46 within 2 km. So we also read each suburb's full list of places, and now show real supermarket names.

$14.8M

That's the last sale on the only record for 23-47 Flinders Street, Surry Hills. It covers the whole building, so updating it would badly mislead a buyer looking at one flat. Whole-building records show no price, just recent sales nearby.

12%

27 of the 227 random addresses we tried had retired IDs. The address list still holds them, but the API refuses them. Some return under a new name: 534D/810 Elizabeth Street is now 534/810. We check an ID is live before offering it.

What our separate check showed

8 of 10

We ran 10 addresses we had not tested, once, on the live server, with no code changes first. None showed facts for a wrong home.

  • C1109/21 Dunkerley Place, Waterloo: the data calls this flat 1109, with no letter. It was offered second on the list.
  • 120-122 Lawson Street, Redfern: this is one house, but the address matcher labelled it a unit, so the panel said "Pick your unit".

What data supported the result?

The Microburbs API was our only data source. It covers about 206,750 home addresses across the City of Sydney's 29 suburbs. For each home the panel uses its address record, basics, sale history, valuation, risk layers, schools, transport, nearby places, street profile, nearby sales and its suburb's median price history.

Addresses come from Open G-NAF. We used neither of the supplied CSV files. Caches stay in memory, and the repo holds no API responses. From listing pages we read only the address.

Matching comes first. "Exact" needs the unit, street number, street and suburb all to agree. Anything less becomes a list to pick from, or a box to type in.

The price is our clever step. It takes the home's own last sale and moves it by how much its suburb's median has moved since:

updated value = sale price × (latest suburb median ÷ suburb median nearest the sale date)

For houses, and for sales over 10 years old, the valuation goes first when one exists. A house only shows the updated price when it falls inside Microburbs' valuation range; otherwise it shows just the last sale. The server keeps the API key, and the extension never sees it.

How did we check it?

  • 10 development addresses guided the matching, and all now behave as expected. We changed one expected result, for 214/219-231 Botany Road, because the data has that building but no unit 214.
  • 10 holdout addresses ran once, and we checked each result against the API's full address list. Answers took 0.4 to 1.6 seconds.
  • Real listings in Chrome worked on both sites: 1001/2 Sam Sing Street on Domain, and 29A Albion Street on realestate.com.au.
  • 200 random homes gave the flag rates, using a fixed seed. Each percentage could be off by up to about 7 points.

What remains uncertain?

  • New Metro stations aren't in the data yet. We found no Waterloo, Barangaroo or Gadigal station. Light rail is mixed in with trains, so the panel's row reads "Rail" and carries a small note.
  • Walking times are straight-line estimates at 80 m a minute, so they can cross water, such as Pyrmont to Balmain.
  • Public housing is an estimate for the small area around a home, not a fact about the home.
  • Price changes are at suburb level, not for one building. Flats have no valuation-range check, so an updated price can still be off. Sales over 10 years old are marked rough.
  • Nearby sales ignore views and penthouses. They match on distance, bedrooms when known and kind of home, so a range can be wide: $706k to $21.50M near 96-98 Elizabeth Bay Road.
  • Coverage is thin for flats. Of the 7 flats in our holdout, 1 had a valuation and 3 had a sale to update. The others show similar nearby sales instead.
  • Dropping a unit's letter is an inference. We treat C1109 as 1109 only when no unit in the building has a letter and exactly one flat has those digits. The panel says so.
  • Some labels are rough. A home with no unit and no type in the data is treated as a flat. "Grocery" covers any shop filed under supermarket without a chain name. Selective schools come from our own short list, under one label, so check each school's entry rules.
  • Nearby shops and parks can be missed just over a suburb boundary, because the full list of places is per suburb.
  • These are small samples. Ten holdout listings can't prove a rate. We also have not rerun the 200-home sample to confirm it repeats exactly.
